# Writ v0.2.0: what changed since v0.1.0, for implementers

v0.1.0 (2026-09-30, commit 6552968) carried 213 vectors and 22 executor scenarios. v0.2.0 carries 290 vectors (70 accepted, 220 rejected) and 25 scenarios. Vectors 1 to 213 keep their numbers and their bytes: every vector since was added at the end. The specification is still `docs/spec/writ-v0.1.md`, and its revisions list, from the entry "2026-09-30, review" onward, is the normative record of what follows. The release also carries the command-line programs; this note covers only what someone building Writ from the text needs.

If your implementation was built against v0.1.0, these are the changes, each with the vectors and scenarios that exercise it.

## 1. A revoke gets a signed reply: the ack

Sections 9.1, 9.4 and 10 (revision "2026-09-30, revocation").

- A fifth signed object, `ack`. An executor that records a revoke signs when it recorded it and exactly which work under the revoked writ it held at that moment.
- The HTTP answer to a revoke is now `{"tallies", "ack", "res", "fwd"}` (section 10). An executor that does not sign acks leaves out members section 10 requires. A sender that does not know acks reads `tallies` exactly as before.
- A revoke of one writ MUST survive restart, as a key-wide revoke already did. A revoke the executor cannot persist is answered with status 503, not as recorded.
- Checking a tally against its signer's ack: forward work the ack does not account for is `signed_unauthorized` with reason `revoked`, however its `acc` is dated. New reason `ack_mismatch`, new vector operation `check_ack` (section 14).

Exercised by vectors 214 to 241, all `check_ack`; scenarios 010 to 015 and 022, whose revoke steps now expect the ack and its body; and new scenarios 023 (a revoke of one writ survives restart) and 024 (an ack holds what finished and lists what runs).

## 2. A sixth bound type: `total`

Sections 3, 6.2, 7.2, 7 step 10, and 9 (revision "2026-10-01, total").

- `max` of X means each charge up to X. `total` of X means X in total, summed over every call one executor accepts under the writ.
- It narrows as `max` does: a child may lower it, but may not raise it, drop it, or turn it into another type. One call's argument is checked against it as `max` is.
- The running sum is executor state, kept per executor in a durable store and consumed at section 7 step 10 beside `count`. A call that would take the sum past X is refused with the new reason `total_exhausted`.
- A tally's `used` reports `total` bounds as well as `max` bounds, and section 6.2 steps 7 and 10 check both.
- A verifier that predates this revision rejects any writ carrying `total` as `unknown_bound`.

Exercised by vectors 242 to 269 and scenario 025 (a total bounds the running sum at each executor).

## 3. Weak keys: small-order Ed25519 keys and signatures

Sections 1.3 and 1.4 (revision "2026-10-04").

- All fourteen encodings of the eight small-order points are `bad_key`, wherever a key appears.
- A signature whose first 32 bytes, its R, are one of those encodings is `bad_signature`.
- Compare the 32 bytes with the top bit of the last byte cleared. A check that forgets to clear it passes seven of the vectors it should reject.
- An implementation that predates this revision accepts all 19 vectors added for it.

Exercised by vectors 270 to 288: a writ, a delegated call, and a sub-tally each signed by the identity-point key with one fixed signature (270 to 272); each of the fourteen encodings as a holder (273 to 286); one in an `hld` bound (287); and an honest key's signature made with the nonce r = 0 (288). No scenario.

## 4. Nesting depth counts containers, not values

Section 1.1 rule 7 (revision "2026-10-01", from issue #22). The wording changed; no verdict did.

- At most 64 levels. The outermost array or object is level 1, and a scalar does not add a level.

Exercised by the new vectors 289 (a scalar inside 64 arrays, accepted) and 290 (a scalar inside 65 arrays, rejected `too_large`), beside 173 to 178. An implementation that gives the scalar a level of its own rejects 289, 176, or both, depending on whether it counts on the raw text, on the parsed value, or on both.

## 5. Clarified, with no new vector or scenario

- Section 9.1: a revoke no longer answers `canceled` for a call not yet accepted. Recording a revoke is atomic with section 7 steps 7 to 11, so such a call is refused at step 7 with a signed `revoked` refusal to its own caller, and no call is left for the revoke to answer (revision "2026-10-01").
- Section 7.6: a peer the transport authenticated but cannot name, such as a verified client certificate naming no peer, is a peer the executor holds no binding for. A binding must not fall back to "no peer" (revision "2026-09-30, review"). Scenario 021 is unchanged.
- Section 9.3: each audit record entry SHOULD carry `prev`, the hash of the entry before it. No check of the protocol consults the record.
- Appendix E (approval as an ordinary delegation) and an x402 row in section 13 are non-normative.

## 6. Corpus tooling

- `conformance/schema/` is new since v0.1.0: JSON schemas for vectors and scenarios. `python scripts/check-schemas.py --control` checks every file against them, and checks the schema's reasons against section 11.
